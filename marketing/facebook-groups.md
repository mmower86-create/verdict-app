# Facebook Groups — the 3rd channel (free, high-intent, less filtered than Reddit)

## Why this one
Your customer (people signing leases, confused/anxious) is ACTIVELY in these
groups. No karma walls like Reddit. More forgiving about helpful posts. Free.

## STEP 1 — Find & join 5-10 groups (search these on Facebook)
- "[Your city] Apartments for Rent" / "[City] Housing"
- "[Nearby college] Off-Campus Housing" / "[College] Sublets & Roommates"
  (college towns = constant lease turnover, esp. now through August = peak season)
- "Renters Rights [your state]" / "Tenants Union [city]"
- "[City] Roommates" / "Moving to [City]"
- Apartment-hunting / first-apartment groups (national ones exist too)

Join them. Some require answering a question to join — answer genuinely.

## STEP 2 — Read the rules of EACH group (pinned post / About section)
- Some ban self-promo entirely → only comment helpfully there, never link.
- Some have a "promo day" or allow value posts → those are your posting targets.
- Same golden rule as Reddit: be helpful FIRST, mention Verdict rarely/softly.

## STEP 3 — Engage before posting (a few days)
- Comment helpfully on lease/deposit questions people ask. NO link.
- When someone asks "is this clause normal?" → answer it well. THIS is where,
  if it genuinely fits and the group allows it, you can say "I actually built a
  free tool that checks leases for this kind of thing, getverdict.net" — once.
- Being the helpful "lease person" in a few groups is the whole play.

## STEP 4 — A value post you can adapt (where groups allow posts)
TITLE/OPENER:
Signing a lease soon? 5 clauses worth checking before you do

BODY:
Renting season is here so figured this might help someone. I read a lot of
leases and these are the ones that quietly cost people money:

1. Repair responsibility with NO dollar cap (a broken water heater = you owe
   thousands). Ask for a cap in writing.
2. Early-termination fees buried deep (often a flat $2-3k, due fast). Find it
   before you sign, not when you're trying to leave.
3. "Landlord may enter for any reason" - most states require 24-48hr notice;
   this is often unenforceable but people don't know to push back.
4. Auto-renewal traps (locks you in for another term if you miss a 60-90 day
   notice window).
5. Security deposit clauses that contradict themselves on the return timeline.

Happy to take a look if anyone wants to paste a weird clause (block out your
personal info). Not legal advice, just patterns I see a lot.

NOTE: same magic as Reddit - the "happy to look" line gets people to engage.
You help in comments. THAT'S where Verdict comes up naturally, if at all.

## What NOT to do (gets you banned from groups fast)
- ❌ Join and immediately post a link. Instant removal + ban.
- ❌ Same copy-paste post in 10 groups at once (FB flags this hard).
- ❌ Comment "use my app!" on everything. You'll get reported.

## The realistic timeline
Week 1: join groups, comment helpfully, no links. Build a tiny reputation.
Week 2+: post value posts where allowed, mention Verdict softly when it fits.
This is still organic = still a slow burn, BUT less filtered than Reddit and the
intent is higher than X. Check Plausible for any FB referral traffic.
